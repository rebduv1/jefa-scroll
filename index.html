The Opportunity

A promise made nearly four thousand years ago — and a new opportunity.

The Educational Choice for Children Act (ECCA) — now known as the Educational Freedom Tax Credit — establishes, for the first time, a federal framework allowing every American taxpayer to direct a portion of the taxes they already owe toward K–12 education.

Beginning with tax year 2027, taxpayers may claim a dollar-for-dollar federal tax credit of up to $1,700 per return for gifts made to approved Scholarship Granting Organizations. This is a credit, not a deduction — money that would otherwise go to the federal government, redirected by the giver.

The question this creates is a simple one: where will those dollars go?

Who We Are

JEFA funds Torah-authentic K–12 education for Orthodox Jewish children.

The Jewish Education Fund of America is a national 501(c)(3) Scholarship Granting Organization. Our scholarships go directly toward funding tuition and qualified school expenses for students of Jewish families raising their children in an authentic Torah education — the very Scriptures held as the foundation of the Creator's revelation on Mount Sinai, taught and transmitted as He gave them.

The need is urgent and, for most Americans, entirely invisible. Orthodox Jewish day school tuition routinely runs $30,000 to $50,000 per family, in communities where large families are common. Working parents — plumbers, teachers, shopkeepers, accountants — are stretched to the breaking point to give their children the Torah education they are required to provide.

When a child's day school education is funded, what is funded is not a building or a campaign — it is the literal transmission of Torah from one generation of the Jewish people to the next.

A Tithe of a Tax Credit

We invite donors to consider dedicating a portion of their annual tax credit to JEFA — 10%, 20%, or more if they feel called to give. The balance remains available for the educational causes already closest to their hearts.

A Partnership, Not a Pledge

What we are asking is more important than money.

We are not asking for a financial commitment today. We are asking whether you would help us listen — to take a brief survey, and to gauge the openness of communities of faith to this kind of partnership. Your honest input, including critical input, is what we most need. The data will shape how we move forward.

  • Whether the Genesis 12:3 framing resonates the way we believe it does
  • What portion of an EFTC credit a community would consider directing toward Jewish education
  • What questions or clarifications would help people engage with confidence
  • Whether the Orthodox, Torah-authentic focus strengthens or complicates the invitation
